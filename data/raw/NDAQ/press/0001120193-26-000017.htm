NEW YORK, October 22, 2026 - Nasdaq, Inc. (Nasdaq: NDAQ) today
reported financial results for the third quarter of 2026.
Adena Friedman, Chair and CEO said,
“Nasdaq’s third quarter performance
demonstrates the strength and
momentum of our business, with new
milestones achieved across all three
divisions, including record Index net
inflows, record FinTech ARR growth, and
the establishment of Nasdaq Digital
Liquidity Networks within Market
Services.
Markets are evolving towards an AI-

classes and geographies. Nasdaq is
shaping the next generation of market
infrastructure while expanding our growth
opportunities and enhancing the value we
deliver to our clients and shareholders.”
•Third quarter 2026 net revenue1 was $1.5 billion, an increase of 13%
over the third quarter of 2025, or 14% on an organic2 basis. Solutions
revenue3 increased 17%, or 18% on an organic basis.
•Annualized Recurring Revenue (ARR)3,4 of $3.4 billion increased 12%
on a reported basis over the third quarter of 2025, or 14% on an organic
basis. Annualized SaaS revenue increased 12%, or 16% on an organic
basis, and represented 38% of ARR.
•Financial Technology revenue was $529 million, an increase of 16% on
both a reported and organic basis over the third quarter of 2025.
•Index revenue of $286 million grew 39% on both a reported and organic
basis over the third quarter of 2025, with $141 billion of net inflows over
the trailing twelve months, including $49 billion in the third quarter of
2026.
•GAAP diluted earnings per share in the third quarter of 2026 was $0.96,
an increase of 31% over the third quarter of 2025. Non-GAAP5 diluted
earnings per share in the third quarter of 2026 was $1.04, an increase of
18% on a reported basis over the third quarter of 2025, or 19% on an
organic basis.
•In the third quarter of 2026, the company returned $173 million to
shareholders through dividends and $323 million through repurchases of
common stock.

FINANCIAL REVIEW
•Third quarter 2026 net revenue was $1.5 billion, reflecting 13% growth on a reported basis
versus the prior year period, or 14% growth on an organic basis.
•Solutions revenue was $1.2 billion in the third quarter of 2026, an increase of 17% versus the
prior year period, or 18% on an organic basis. Performance in the quarter reflected strong
growth across Capital Access Platforms and Financial Technology. Capital Access Platforms
revenue growth was 20% year-over-year on both a reported and organic basis. Financial
Technology revenue growth was 16%, on both a reported and organic basis.
•ARR was $3.4 billion as of the third quarter of 2026, growing 12% year-over-year on a
reported basis, or 14% on an organic basis. The difference between total reported and organic
ARR growth results from the impact of divestitures, which were excluded from ARR in the prior
year period. Financial Technology ARR growth was 18% on both a reported and organic basis,
and Capital Access Platforms ARR growth was 8% on both a reported and organic basis.
•Market Services net revenue was $309 million in the third quarter of 2026, up 2% on both a
reported and organic basis versus the prior year period.
•Third quarter 2026 GAAP operating expenses were $749 million, an increase of 3% versus
the prior year quarter. Non-GAAP operating expenses were $638 million, up 9% versus the
prior year quarter, or 12% on an organic basis. The increases were primarily driven by higher
compensation and benefits costs from our strong revenue execution and increased
investments in technology to drive long-term growth. On a GAAP basis, the increase was
partially offset by lower general and administrative expenses, primarily due to a favorable legal
settlement, and lower restructuring charges.  
•Cash flow from operations was $521 million in the third quarter, enabling the return of capital
through Nasdaq’s efficient capital allocation framework. In the third quarter of 2026, the
company returned $173 million to shareholders through dividends and $323 million through
repurchases of common stock. As of September 30, 2026, there was $2.2 billion remaining
under the board authorized share repurchase program.
2026 EXPENSE AND TAX GUIDANCE UPDATE6
•The company is updating its 2026 non-GAAP operating expense guidance to a range of
$2.550 billion to $2.580 billion. The company is maintaining its 2026 non-GAAP tax rate
guidance in the range of 22.5% to 24.5%.

STRATEGIC AND BUSINESS UPDATES
•Financial Technology delivered record 18% organic ARR growth and double-digit
revenue growth in each subdivision for the third consecutive quarter. FinTech signed 54
new clients and 135 upsells in the quarter with cloud-based solutions accounting for 85% of
ACV bookings. FinTech performance reflected continued execution on the One Nasdaq
strategy with 5 cross-sells signed in the quarter and cross-sells accounting for over 15% of the
sales pipeline. Nasdaq achieved a second-place ranking on the Chartis RiskTech100, up from
fifth place in the prior year.
◦Financial Crime Management Technology revenue and ARR growth accelerated
with momentum across SMBs and enterprise clients. Nasdaq Verafin signed 45
new small-and-medium bank (SMB) clients and 3 enterprise deals in the third quarter,
bringing year-to-date enterprise signings to 12, surpassing the 9 enterprise deals
signed in all of 2025. Nasdaq Verafin announced the general availability of two new AI
workers, the Agentic AML Analyst and the Agentic Fraud Analyst, and now has more
than 850 clients using its Agentic AI Workforce.
◦Regulatory Technology achieved the fifth straight quarter of accelerating ARR
growth led by strong performance across Surveillance and AxiomSL. The
subdivision added 10 new clients, including 2 cross-sells, and 86 upsells in the third
quarter, driven by demand for cloud infrastructure, AI, digital assets, and regulatory
requirements. Surveillance signed 9 new clients, including 2 cross-sells and strategic
wins with Kalshi and Kraken, while also achieving 51 upsells. AxiomSL added 1 new
client and 35 upsells in the third quarter, including an expansion with a Global
Systemically Important Bank (G-SIB) for a Basel III implementation.
◦Capital Markets Technology delivered robust organic ARR growth with broad-
based strength across the subdivision. The subdivision signed 2 new clients,
including 1 cross-sell, and 48 upsells in the third quarter. Calypso added 2 new clients,
including 1 cross-sell, and 23 upsells. Nasdaq partnered with HSBC to co-develop
comprehensive exchange-traded derivatives (ETD) clearing capabilities in Calypso,
creating a unified platform HSBC has selected for clearing across OTC, ETD, and
repos. Nasdaq also launched a new operating environment which enables clients to
connect their own AI infrastructure and serve as an access point for Calypso’s agentic
capabilities. Market Technology signed 25 upsells in the quarter and continued to
shape Always-On markets, signing a deal with Bruce Markets to power the first 24/7
continuous trading in U.S. equities.
•Index set new ETP assets under management (AUM) and net inflow records while
executing on its growth pillars of product innovation, international expansion, and
institutional adoption. Index had a record $141 billion in net inflows over the trailing twelve
months, including $49 billion in the third quarter. End-of-period ETP AUM was $1.160 trillion
and average ETP AUM over the third quarter was $1.108 trillion, both new records. Nasdaq
launched 32 new Index products in the third quarter, including 10 in the institutional annuity
space. Half of the new products launched in the third quarter were outside the U.S.
4
•Nasdaq extended its listings leadership with 8 of the 10 largest operating company
IPOs in the third quarter. Nasdaq welcomed 15 new operating companies that raised $32
billion in capital7, reflecting a 79% win rate8 of new operating company listings. The quarter’s
strong performance contributed to a 74% year-to-date new listings win rate. Year-to-date
through the third quarter, new operating company listings raised $144 billion in proceeds,
reflecting 89% of total proceeds raised.
•Nasdaq expanded its distribution of private markets data through a partnership with
Juniper Square. The partnership embeds eVestment's private markets data directly into
Juniper Square’s client workflows, strengthening Nasdaq's position as a trusted provider of
institutional investment intelligence. eVestment also expanded its coverage of private funds to
more than 95,000, a 19% increase from the end of 2025.
•Nasdaq executed several strategic actions to advance the evolution of capital markets.
Nasdaq formed Digital Liquidity Networks (DLN), a new organization within Nasdaq Market
Services focused on advancing the company’s leadership in digital assets and market
modernization. Within DLN, Nasdaq completed the acquisition of LeveL Markets, the third-
largest Alternative Trading System (ATS) in the U.S. by trading volume with more than 2,500
buy-side and sell-side clients. The acquisition, which closed early in the fourth quarter,
enhances Nasdaq’s ability to serve a wider liquidity pool across off-exchange venues and
deliver institutional-grade liquidity and execution solutions to clients.
•Market Services set a new record in third quarter net revenue and continued to lead the
evolution of markets including the transition to Always-On markets. Third quarter Market
Services net revenue benefitted from higher industry volumes across U.S. options and
European equities with robust performance in U.S. cash equities, which achieved higher
market share and near record capture. The Nasdaq Stock Market remains on track to go live
with 23/5 trading on December 6, 2026, expanding global access to U.S.-listed equities.

NON-GAAP INFORMATION
In addition to disclosing results determined in accordance with U.S. GAAP, Nasdaq also discloses certain
non-GAAP results of operations, including, but not limited to, non-GAAP net income, non-GAAP diluted
earnings per share, non-GAAP operating income, and non-GAAP operating expenses, that include certain
adjustments or exclude certain charges and gains that are described in the reconciliation tables of U.S.
GAAP to non-GAAP information provided at the end of this release. Management uses this non-GAAP
information internally, along with U.S. GAAP information, in evaluating our performance and in making
financial and operational decisions. We believe our presentation of these measures provides investors
with greater transparency and supplemental data relating to our financial condition and results of
operations. In addition, we believe the presentation of these measures is useful to investors for period-to-
period comparisons of results as the items described below in the reconciliation tables do not reflect
ongoing operating performance.
These measures are not in accordance with, or an alternative to, U.S. GAAP, and may be different from
non-GAAP measures used by other companies. In addition, other companies, including companies in our
industry, may calculate such measures differently, which reduces their usefulness as a comparative
measure. Investors should not rely on any single financial measure when evaluating our business. This
information should be considered as supplemental in nature and is not meant as a substitute for our
operating results in accordance with U.S. GAAP. We recommend investors review the U.S. GAAP
financial measures included in this earnings release. When viewed in conjunction with our U.S. GAAP
results and the accompanying reconciliations, we believe these non-GAAP measures provide greater
transparency and a more complete understanding of factors affecting our business than U.S. GAAP
measures alone.
We understand that analysts and investors regularly rely on non-GAAP financial measures, such as those
noted above, to assess operating performance. We use these measures because they highlight trends
more clearly in our business that may not otherwise be apparent when relying solely on U.S. GAAP
financial measures, since these measures eliminate from our results specific financial items that have less
bearing on our ongoing operating performance.
Foreign exchange impact: In countries with currencies other than the U.S. dollar, revenue and expenses
are translated using monthly average exchange rates. Certain discussions in this release isolate the
impact of year-over-year foreign currency fluctuations to better measure the comparability of operating
results between periods. Operating results excluding the impact of foreign currency fluctuations are
calculated by translating the current period’s results by the prior period’s exchange rates.

Annualized Recurring Revenue (ARR) for a given period is the current annualized value derived from subscription
contracts with a defined contract value. This excludes contracts that are not recurring, are one-time in nature, or
where the contract value fluctuates based on defined metrics. ARR is currently one of our key performance metrics
to assess the health and trajectory of our recurring business. ARR does not have any standardized definition and is
therefore unlikely to be comparable to similarly titled measures presented by other companies. ARR should be
viewed independently of revenue and deferred revenue and is not intended to be combined with or to replace either
of those items. For AxiomSL and Calypso recurring revenue contracts, the amount included in ARR is consistent with
the amount that we invoice the customer during the current period. Additionally, for AxiomSL and Calypso recurring
revenue contracts that include annual values that increase over time, we include in ARR only the annualized value of
components of the contract that are considered active as of the date of the ARR calculation. We do not include the
future committed increases in the contract value as of the date of the ARR calculation. ARR is not a forecast and the
active contracts at the end of a reporting period used in calculating ARR may or may not be extended or renewed by
our customers.
